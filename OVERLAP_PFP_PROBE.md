# Boundary-complete representatives: small independent construction gate

This tests a different representation, not an optimization of `unit.rs`. The Rust ladder remains the byte-semantics baseline, not evidence for the new representation. Reproduce with `python3 gates/overlap_pfp_oracle.py --corpus /mnt/nvme2n1/erikg/TAPEREADER/sandbox/pile-slice100.bin`.

Choose anchors by a fixed predicate on each complete w-gram, independent of previous boundaries. Overlapping anchors are allowed. Add start 0. Tile [a,b) owns exactly [a,b); its phrase includes the w bytes starting at b. The last phrase ends in a unique minimal EOF instead (an integer outside the byte alphabet, not byte 0). For i in [a,b), the representative is phrase[i-a:]. The empty suffix is output separately at rank 0.

Why no proper prefix: a non-final representative ends in the first trigger w-gram starting **strictly after** its row's start. If it were a proper prefix of a longer representative, that same w-gram would trigger at the same positive offset in the longer one, forcing it to end there too. A final representative contains EOF and can't be a proper prefix. This is about representatives, not merely whole phrases. A fixed forced split violates the proof (even an all-`a` string gives overlapping tails `aaa` and `aaaa`). An I/O chunk boundary does not invalidate it provided the rolling trigger scan continues through it.

Unequal representatives sort in dictionary order. For equal representatives ending at b+w, both suffixes share the first w bytes of their respective continuations starting at b, so integer **next-parse-suffix rank** is sufficient. No per-row comparison against a boundary, arrival files, or suffix landing. For LCP between unequal keys, use their dictionary LCP. For tied keys of length L, use `L-w + aligned_lcp(next_a,next_b)`; the aligned LCP sums tile lengths for equal phrase IDs, then takes the dictionary LCP of the first unequal phrase IDs. The small implementation uses an integer prefix-doubling SA and simple ID walks, not optimized LCP/RMQ. The brute text SA is used only to check the result.

Small gate: 2044 exhaustive input/trigger combinations plus 7 named fixtures; prefix-freeness checked on all distinct representatives, every suffix position and LCP matched brute including EOF. At 1504 bytes on the repeat fixture, 25 representative keys and 1479 tied rows; at the 4096-byte real corpus head, 3941 distinct representatives and 155 tied rows. This is **not** evidence that dictionary sorting is cheap. Period-5 and all-`A` examples had no interior trigger for this predicate. The proof tolerates this but the naïve materialized-tail oracle can cost quadratic space. Do not use it on large inputs. Logical document ends also need a separate boundary policy before a multi-document product claim.

Original product-facing gate design: store representatives as (phrase,offset,length) references; sort the dictionary tails without copying quadratic suffix bytes (e.g. build a suffix order on the dictionary with correct finite-key endpoints), group equal reps and merge their occurrence lists by integer rank. Compare the resulting row stream **and LCP** against the current certified mixed fixture before touching finish wiring. Measure dictionary bytes, distinct reps, longest untriggered span, and physical reads under a cache cap; correctness alone is not the locality goal. In particular, do not put a byte-comparison sort of corpus references in the emitter and call it an integer emit.
