# One resident PFP comparison (prepared 1 GB lexparse)

Question: is the 96-minute external emission inherent to the order, or the cost of repeatedly reconstructing local working sets? Hold the text, overlap convention, and row/finish oracles fixed. Give the build 20 GiB shared (no swap, 4 CPU quota, 2 finish threads); reserve 14 GiB for resident producer arrays. Keep the old external gate unchanged. Falsifiable prediction: both sorts fit separately, emission needs **zero dictionary scans from disk**, and elapsed time from the prepared IDs/refs through the 1 GB container is less than the old *emission alone*. No implication that a signed-32-bit kernel or these density ratios work at 10 GB or the Pile.

* Input: only `parse.u32` (m IDs), `dictionary.refs` (k position/length/EOF pairs), corpus, remap. No precomputed order, alignments, lists, keys or windows. A **check-only** private copy of `inverse.u32` verifies each rank; no value from it participates in construction. `libsais_int` takes the flat m integers (not the text, and no second parse at this rung). Give it 6k extra i32 slots in its SA buffer per its workspace contract, then free them.
* Read the corpus sequentially into an anonymous byte array. Encode used raw bytes in monotone byte codes 2..255 (explicit refusal if not enough codes); dictionary = each distinct word once, with code 1 for virtual END only on the final word, 0 separator after each word. For `z` identical phrase IDs, byte LCP at a parse boundary = displacement across those `z` strides + finite LCP at the first unequal word. Get `z` from the integer PLCP. Build occurrence lists in successor-SA order, with separate text position, next rank and external predecessor. Drop corpus, parse and parse SA.
* `libsais` sorts the D dictionary **byte positions** once. There is no raw corpus SA/PLCP column at any point. A u32 owner map discards separator/overlap-only positions. Compare consecutive surviving finite tails directly in resident bytes, not their concatenated suffixes beyond the separator. Different boundary-complete tails are prefix-free; equal tails are merged on next rank, with byte LCP `len - 8 + aligned_RMQ`. Long/skewed words are a counterexample to treating direct LCP comparisons as constant cost: the sum of finite key lengths bounds the bytes matched (40.925 GB of *logical* keys on this rung), not the physical IO. No copies of those keys. Equal keys have at most one offset per word; the plan includes space even for a doubled heap of 2k cursors.
* Account for overlapping lifetimes, not summed phase peaks. Parse bound `8m + 8k + 24k` bytes including refs/workspace; packing/list bound `N + D + 20k + 32m`. During sort, `5D` bytes for dictionary and SA; during emission `9D` including owner. Both retain offsets/CSR/RMQ (~`8k + 13.5m`), with explicit allowance for library work and the merge heap. Refuse if the maximum plan exceeds the producer grant. Kernel uses signed 32-bit lengths, output u32 triples; n, m, k and D checked before sorts. The sandbox fixture paths have **not** had their caps raised.
* Stream LE triples to the Rust piece/LCP cutter and a separately capped raw verifier. No triples file. Start sink/finish at emission; times overlap. Measure `read_bytes/write_bytes` per worker and producer phase, RSS (own VmHWM reset per phase), shared memory peak, cgroup IO and time blocked writing both pipes. The run starts after lexical parsing; copies/eviction, oracle work and post-run hash are separate. Frozen finish in its usual v8 nine-member profile. Scratch plan reserves the retained IDs/refs, sink backlog, fixed-grid PFCK/bitmap and the known profile's finish spools before emitting. Count actual scratch including nested pieces/slices; distinguish sampled whole-directory allocated disk, input/check copies and the partial/final product.

Reproduce: `bash gates/overlap_resident_gate.sh`. First `gates/overlap_resident_test.py` compares every triple on 18 small inputs including NUL, EOF at a trigger, ties and repetitive text; checks wrong-rank, byte-alphabet and undersized-grant refusals and stop=75. The measured arm uses a new directory and private cold inodes (`mincore` zero), never evicts shared files. On a failure keep the receipt; do not advance the rung.

## Result at 1 GB: full container passed

`gates/receipts/resident-1g/` (`resident-rung1-a1ru0L`, code `143ade3`), with the phase and worker [table](gates/receipts/resident-1g/summary.md). Every one of 31,784,026 parse ranks matched. Raw verifier checked all 1,000,000,666 streamed triples and the final suffix rank. Frozen finish published 1,014,098,520 bytes, SHA `b20ceebe91707dbac80c2ea12c99e704c6b6a734293f3a558c90ce090c21f6a3`, identical v8 nine-member table. No OOM or stop. The first harness invocation (`resident-rung1-bADpOh`) failed **before construction**: no delegated cgroup `io.stat`, and reopening a zombie's `/proc/pid/io` was denied. On the actual arm worker IO FDs were opened while live; no cgroup IO totals are imputed.

From prepared IDs/refs to completed container: **465.669 s**, including writes to both consumers; not time from raw text. Parse sort 2.981 s; D=1,166,209,205 dictionary positions sorted in 47.353 s; emit 381.914 s (33.600 s writing the product pipe, 77.334 s writing the audit pipe). Sink and finish overlapped emission; do not subtract either wall time to infer an unaudited speed. Producer read 1.457 GB incl. 127 MB rank check and 1 GB corpus; no attributed reads during either sort or emission. Build workers wrote 9.290 GB in total (proc accounting, not live scratch). Separate oracle used 84.09 CPU s and read 1 GB. Producer RSS peaked at 11.13 GB in emit; shared cgroup peak 13.142 GiB / 20 GiB. Counted scratch (including 329,829,376 B of IDs/refs) 1,119,529,213 B sampled, 1,597,106,334 B conservatively reserved / 2,000,001,330 B limit; whole private directory 3,797,041,152 B sampled allocated, including oracle copy and output. Compare 381.914 s to 5,760.749 s for the old windowed route/join/emit from precomputed columns. These are different grants and sinks, not a controlled CPU speedup. No 3, 10, 100 GB or Pile verdict on this path.

## What if the parse exceeds a 32-bit sort?

Width is a property of a column, not the corpus as a whole. Let N be corpus bytes, m parse occurrences, k distinct words, D encoded dictionary symbols. N controls byte offsets/LCP bounds; m controls suffix indices, continuation ranks and CSR endpoints; k controls lexical IDs; D controls dictionary suffix indices. Also check sentinel/flag and end-exclusive values, the largest word and the `n + fs` extent before calling a specific kernel. A signed-i32 kernel can refuse when an on-disk u32 column is still representable. Keep 64-bit global coordinates/counts and bounded local ordinals; widen the row transport and references with the sinks, not just the SA function. Upstream [libsais64.h](https://github.com/IlyaGrebnov/libsais/blob/master/include/libsais64.h) exposes `libsais64` for bytes and `libsais64_long` for integers. Neither is vendored/gated here. Integer input and SA in the latter are both int64; don't budget it as a u32 sort. If **only** width fails and the live set fits, use the wide kernel (first force it on the 1 GB oracle), not another parse. For D alone over width, a separated lexical-range sort of word tails with global tie groups is another option; no reason to recurse on m.

**Small falsifiable study, not a merge benchmark.** If a RopeBWT-style parse merger depends on run compression, r/m should be small. If a second overlapping parse saves the *whole* lower-level sort by the same factor as its token shrinkage, the mass of the new dictionary should fall comparably. `bash gates/overlap_parse_space.sh` on the entire 127 MB P1 from the rung (1 GiB/no swap). Scatter preceding IDs to their independently verified 1-based ranks, use the real last token at END rank 0 and a unique BWT sentinel. Reparse P1 on token-aligned 8-ID trigger windows, never on the byte serialization; compare actual token sequences to assign distinct P2 words. [Receipt](gates/receipts/parse-space-1g/result.log):

* P1 has **28,654,688 runs / 31,784,027 BWT rows (90.15%)**. The 25,336,659 distinct IDs alone force at least 25,336,660 runs including END. Not evidence for a small run-length rope. Different batched rank structures and workloads remain possible.
* P2 has **1,023,982 tokens, 4,095,928 bytes u32, 31.04× shorter**, but 1,018,343 distinct P2 words; its BWT must have at least 99.45% as many runs as rows. Its dictionary has 40,856,840 encoded **token** positions (including overlap, END and separators) and owns **31,691,760** P1 offsets, 99.71% of m. Recursion makes the *top* integer sort small, not the induction or lower continuation metadata. No P2 induction/product claimed by this census.
* A genuine single-text block merger needs the true text continuation across artificial cuts, globally comparable IDs, and a way to emit (parse ordinal, global suffix rank) and aligned byte LCP, not only a collection BWT with local sentinels. For example, split `[a,a | b]` with `a < b`: the left block alone sorts position 1 (`a`) before 0 (`aa`), but in the real text position 0 (`aab`) sorts before 1 (`ab`). Even the *internal* order of one cropped block can be wrong; a stable interleave of its locally sorted rows cannot fix that. Use a unique END and a tested concatenation/continuation-aware merge before considering speed. The existing checked external-doubling recurrence is a simpler exact backend to widen if neither resident sorting nor useful recursion fits. Its *measured* implementation and finish/stream joins are still limited to rung widths; there is no certified arbitrary-64-bit fallback yet.

Next decision before the 3 GB gate: size D independently of m; test width conversions with tiny artificial ceilings and high-base coordinates (not a 2^31-element mock allocation). At the observed density D would hit signed i32 around 1.84 GB of corpus vs ~67.6 GB for m; projections are not censuses. Benchmark a wide dictionary kernel against the checked 1 GB rows before changing parse strategy. If parsing twice is needed, verify induced P1 ranks and aligned LCP against the existing oracle under a forced threshold. Measure total passes and both dictionaries, not just P2 size.
